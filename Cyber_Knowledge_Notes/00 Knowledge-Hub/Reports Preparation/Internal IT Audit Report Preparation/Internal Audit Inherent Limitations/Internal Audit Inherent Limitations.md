##### What does “Internal Audit Inherent Limitations” mean?
In simple words:  
It is a disclaimer that explains what an internal audit can and cannot do.
Internal audit does its best, but it cannot check everything, all the time, with 100% certainty. This section clearly sets that expectation.

##### Why is this written in the audit report?
This section exists to:
1. Set realistic expectations for management and the board
2. Protect the auditor from being blamed for things that were impossible to detect
3. Explain how the audit was performed and its natural limits
4. Clarify that management owns the risks, not the auditor

Think of it like saying:

> “We reviewed what we reasonably could, based on time, data, and access, but this is not a guarantee that no issues exist.”
---

### Let’s break your text into very simple meaning
##### 1. “We relied on interviews, documents, observations, and samples”

This means:
- Auditors talked to people
- Reviewed documents and records
- Observed activities
- Tested samples, not everything

👉 Auditors do not monitor systems 24/7 and do not test every transaction.

##### 2️. “Sample testing was judgmental”
This means:
- Auditors selected some examples, not all
- Selection was based on professional judgment and time available
👉 Because of this, some issues may exist that were not seen.

##### 3. “Reasonable assurance, not absolute assurance”
This is the most important line.
- Reasonable assurance = “High confidence, but not 100%”
- Absolute assurance = “Guaranteed, nothing can go wrong” ❌ (this is impossible)
👉 Internal audit reduces risk, it does not eliminate risk.

##### 4️. “Management is responsible for risks”
This means:
- Auditors identify and report risks
- Management owns, accepts, and fixes risks
👉 Audit does not run IT, security, or operations — management does.


##### 5️. “We are not responsible for changes after fieldwork”
This means:
- Audit findings are valid only up to the audit end date
- If something changed later, it’s outside audit responsibility

##### 6️. “This report is confidential”
This means:
- The report is for SCA management only
- It cannot be shared externally without permission

## Why is this VERY important legally and professionally?

###### Without this section:
- Management could say:  
_“Why didn’t the audit catch this breach?”_

- Regulators could say:  
_“Audit approved everything.”_


###### This section clearly says:

> **Audit is not a guarantee. It is an independent review based on limited time, access, and samples.**

Think of internal audit like a **doctor’s check-up**:

- Doctor cannot guarantee you’ll never get sick
- Doctor advises improvements
- **You** are responsible for your health

Same with audit.

## One Line Summary:
Internal audit inherent limitations explain that the audit provides reasonable assurance based on sample testing and available information, but it cannot guarantee that all risks or issues have been identified.

---
## Summary:
The Internal Audit Inherent Limitations section is included to clarify that the audit was performed based on available information, interviews, documentation review, physical observations, and sample testing within a defined time period, and therefore provides reasonable rather than absolute assurance. As the audit does not examine every transaction, system, or control, and does not account for changes occurring after fieldwork, some risks or issues may remain undetected. This section also emphasizes that while internal audit independently assesses and reports on control effectiveness, the responsibility for managing risks, maintaining controls, and ensuring compliance ultimately remains with management.
